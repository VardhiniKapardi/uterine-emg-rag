Question:
What are the main characteristics of uterine electromyography (EMG) signals?

Gold papers:
paper1, paper2, paper3, paper4, paper5

Retrieved papers:
paper2, paper2, paper2, paper2, paper2

Retrieved chunk IDs:
25, 55, 44, 45, 87

Answer:
The main characteristics of uterine EMG signals include:

1. **Low Amplitude**: Typically ranging from a few tens of microvolts to about 1 millivolt on the abdominal surface.
2. **Frequency Content**: Low-frequency, mainly under 5 Hz, with significant power in the 50–150 Hz range for skeletal muscle EMG.
3. **Waveform Characteristics**: Low-frequency, long-duration bursts rather than brief spikes typical of skeletal muscle EMG.
4. **Propagation Mechanism**: Propagation via a coupled uterine syncytium, originating from smooth-muscle action potentials.
5. **Signal Components**: Comprising slow baseline shifts and fast-wave components up to ~1 Hz.
6. **Temporal Pattern**: Manifests as rhythmic slow depolarizations with superimposed action-potential spikes at the cellular level, and as compound bursts at the myometrial-tissue level.
7. **Labor Progression**: Amplitude increases during labor due to stronger and more synchronized contractions.

Answer:
Uterine EMG signals are generated by low-frequency bursts of long-duration, calcium-mediated action potentials. These action potentials are dominant energy below approximately 1 Hz and are not generated by brief, high-frequency interference-pattern activity like skeletal muscle EMG. Instead, they reflect the intrinsic pacemaker activity of uterine smooth muscle, which is self-excitable and influenced by hormonal and paracrine factors.

The major limitations of uterine EMG or EHG as a method for pregnancy and labor assessment include:

2. **Signal Quality vs. Invasiveness**: Internal electrodes yield cleaner, higher amplitude recordings but pose risks and are limited to research. Surface electrodes are safer but produce attenuated and noisy signals, requiring robust signal processing.

5. **Technical Barriers**: Recording good EHG requires careful skin preparation, stable electrodes, and advanced signal processing techniques. Poor signal-to-noise ratios can lead to missed or false contraction detections, particularly early in pregnancy.

Query 30

Question:
What challenges affect the reliability and reproducibility of uterine EMG measurements?

Gold papers:
paper1, paper2, paper3, paper4, paper5

Retrieved papers:
paper2, paper2, paper2, paper2, paper6

Retrieved chunk IDs:
62, 51, 38, 7, 31

Answer:
The reliability and reproducibility of uterine EMG measurements are affected by several challenges:

1. **Signal Quality vs. Invasiveness Trade-off**: Noninvasive surface EHG is safer but attenuated and noise-prone compared to invasive internal electrodes, which can lead to cleaner but riskier recordings.

2. **Contamination**: The abdominal surface carries multiple signals including maternal and fetal ECG, respiration, fetal skeletal muscle EMG, and motion artifacts. This makes it difficult to isolate the uterine signal, requiring advanced filtering techniques.

3. **Comfort and Reproducibility Issues**: These make it challenging to obtain consistent and reliable measurements in a clinical setting.

4. **Interference from Pelvic Floor Muscles**: Fetal pelvic floor muscle EMG can interfere with uterine EMG signals, complicating their interpretation.

5. **Signal Attenuation**: The abdominal signal is small and easily buried in noise, making it harder to achieve good recordings.

6. **Variability Between and Within Women**: There is significant variation in signals between and within women due to factors like adipose thickness, electrode placement, parity, and gestational age, making it hard to establish absolute thresholds.

7. **Complexity of High-Density Mapping Systems**: Advanced multi-electrode mapping systems remain clinically impractical due to their complexity and computational demands.
